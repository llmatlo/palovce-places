Zgornje Palovče — SP House Settlement Diagram

Eight houses, 1827 cadastral survey | Parish of Vranja Peč / Kamnik
Zgornje Palovče ("Superior Palovče") was a tiny settlement of roughly eight houses in the hills above Kamnik. The 1827 Habsburg cadastral survey assigned house numbers SP1–SP8, though SP7 and SP8 may not have been officially surveyed. Note: House numbers shifted by one digit around 1800 — pre-1800 records reference numbers one higher than the 1827 assignments. Each house below shows the family succession from earliest records through the late 1800s, with cross-links showing marriages between houses.

Cross-House Marriages & Family Connections

SP4 → SP3
Helena Rems (b. 1777, SP3/4) m. Georg Osredkar 1796. Rems house → Osredkar ownership. The central event in Gary's research.
SP4 → SP1
Franz Rems (of Gregor, SP4) m. Maria Vaupetič (SP1). By 1827 Franz owns SP1 — a Rems takes the Vaupetič house.
SP4 → SP2
Ursula Primi Rems (daughter of Primus, SP4) m. Michael Vaup (SP2) ~1770s. Their son Georgius owned SP2 in 1827.
SP5 → SP3
Francizka Koschir (SP5, daughter of Joseph & Gertrud Kladnik) m. Budnar 1874. Budnar family takes over SP3 — it becomes the museum.
SP3 ? SP4
The Two-Martin question: If Martin (SP3) is son of Lucas & Helena (SP4), then SP3 and SP4 share Lucas Rems as common ancestor. If son of Sebastian, they diverge before the 1620s.
Sovinja Peč → SP3
Georg Osredkar (b. 1777, Liukovo) arrived 1796 to marry Helena Rems. ~2 hours walk. His brother Joseph kept the family homestead.
Bela Peč → SP1/2
Maria Osredkar (b. 1777, Bela Peč 3) m. Joseph Vaupetič 1796. Same year as Georg — both born 1777, both arrived at Palovče in 1796.
Kregarjevo → SP4
Helena Kregar m. Lucas Rems 1693. Earliest Kregar link. Her descendant Maria Kregar also married into Rems (SP3).
Kregarjevo → SP3
Maria Kregar (great-niece of Helena) m. Mathias Rems 1776. Mother of Helena Rems — two generations of Kregar women married Rems men.
Inf. Palovče → SP5
Joseph Koschir m. Maria Rems (b. 1810, SP5) 1826. Married in; owned SP5 by 1827. Outsider surname entering the settlement.

Key Patterns

Rems dominance: The Rems family appears in SP3, SP4, SP5, SP8 and married into SP1 and SP2. By the 1700s they were the most widespread family in the settlement, with branches at nearly every house.
The 1796 double arrival: Both Georg Osredkar (from Sovinja Peč) and Maria Osredkar (from Bela Peč) married into Palovče in 1796 — the same year, from the same general area, both born in 1777. Whether this was coincidence or arranged remains an open question.
SP3 as nexus: SP3 (the Budnar Museum) is the genealogical crossroads of the entire research. Five family lines converge here: Osredkar, Rems, Kregar, and through connections to other houses, Vaupetič and Pistotnik.
Cross-house marriages: SP4 Rems married into SP1 (Franz), SP2 (Ursula), and SP3 (Helena). The Rems family network connected nearly every house in the settlement through marriage.
Vulgo names track houses, not blood: SA records name houses ("Matjas House," "Šuštar," "Pirc House"), and families took the house's vulgo name when they moved in. The "Rems" house (SP5) was owned by a Koschir by 1827.
Outsiders arriving: Martin Oraschim (SP6) has no known connections to earlier SP families. Joseph Koschir (SP5) came from Inferior Palovče. The Dollar family (SP7) came from Trobeum. These arrivals in the early 1800s mark a shift from the older Rems/Vaupetič/Urankar network.
