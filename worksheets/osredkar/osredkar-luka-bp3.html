Husband Notes

  • RESOLVED 2026-08-02: the Bela Pec Osredkar line is NOT separate from the SP5/Liukovim Osredkar line — Luka’s father Gregor was confirmed to be the same man as SP5’s Gregorius Osredkar (Georg’s ancestor). See full resolution in osredkar-G08-gregorius-marina.md. Luka founded the Bela Peč/Osredka homestead as one of Gregorius’s sons; he did not belong to a separate patriarch.
  • In a 1705 baptismal witness record, his name was written “Luka Gregor Osredkar” — the patronymic confirms he is Gregor’s (=Gregorius’s) son
  • Caveat: Luka’s own birth/baptismal record naming his parents has still never been located. The parentage link rests on the uncited compiled document pistotniki-osredkarji, whose reliability was checked by cross-verifying three of Luka’s listed “siblings” (Martin, Ursula, Agnes) against real Matricula citations in Gary’s own SP5 research — all three checked out as genuine, lending credibility to the same document’s claim about Luka, but this is still an inference, not a directly-sourced fact.
  • Luka’s father Gregorius died 9 Oct 1727 at ~age 70 (age at death per the SP5-side death record; the earlier “~age 60” figure came from the now-superseded assumption that Gregor was a separate, younger man born 1667)

Children Notes

  • Devastating mortality: 5 of 6 children died before adulthood. Only Elizabeta survived to marry.
  • Simon and Sebastian both died in their early 20s (1724 and 1727) — possible epidemic or accident
  • Baby Marija lived only 5 days
  • Helena died at 14; baby Gregor at 11 months
  • Elizabeta (b. 1710) was the only child to marry and have descendants — she married Joseph Pistotnik from Gabria in 1730, establishing the Pistotnik/Osredkar dual identity at Bela Pec 3
  • There is a 7-year gap between Helena (1714) and Gregor (1721) — additional children may exist in unexamined records
  • Luka outlived all but one of his children; by his death in 1754, only son-in-law Joseph Pistotnik and granddaughter Elizabeta’s children carried on the farm

Ancestry Notes

  • Bartolomej Osredkar (b. ~1647) is the earliest tentatively-identified ancestor — still uncited, still effectively a brick wall pending Gregorius’s own birth record
  • Gregorius/Gregor had 7 known children, not 5 — the merge revealed Luka and Elizabet as full siblings of Martin, Valentin, Ursula, Joannes, and Agnes, who were previously tracked only on the SP5 side. Luka appears to be the eldest based on estimated birth date.
  • Luka’s brother Martin (b. 1684) + Margareta had daughter Helena (b. 1705) — a collateral line
  • Luka’s brother Joannes (b. 1691) is Georg Osredkar’s direct ancestor — meaning Georg and Maria (who both married into Zgornje Palovče in 1796) are more closely related than previously tracked: Georg descends from Joannes, Maria descends from Luka, both sons of the same Gregorius/Marina.
  • The naming “Luka Gregor Osredkar” (patronymic) confirms the father-son link

SIGNIFICANCE

  1. Last male Osredkar at Bela Pec 3: With all sons dead, the Osredkar name at BP3 continued only through son-in-law Joseph Pistotnik’s adoption of the house name
  2. Pistotnik/Osredkar origin: Elizabeta’s 1730 marriage to Joseph Pistotnik created the dual identity “Pistotnik fue Osredkar” that persisted for generations
  3. Connection to Vaupetic line: Maria Osredkar (b. ~1775, Bela Pec 3) who married Joseph Vaupetic descended from this Pistotnik/Osredkar line — she was NOT a blood Osredkar but carried the name through the farm
  4. NOT separate from the SP5 line (resolved 2026-08-02): The Bela Pec Osredkar line (Bartolomej → Gregorius → Luka) shares its patriarch directly with the SP5/Liukovim line (Gregorius → Joannes → Martinus → Georg) — Luka and Joannes are brothers, both sons of Gregorius/Marina. See osredkar-G08-gregorius-marina.md for the full resolution.
  5. Resolved 2026-08-02: what was flagged as a possible duplicate-person question turned out to be exactly that — this worksheet’s “Gregor” and SP5’s Gregorius are the same man, independently transcribed into two Acorn records that have now been merged (hypothesis c3bda747, confidence: probable). The identical death date, matching wife’s name, and three children sharing name+birth year (Martin 1684, Ursula 1688, Agnes 1695) all turned out to be the same real, primary-sourced facts appearing twice, not coincidence. Luka’s own birth record still hasn’t been found, so his specific place in the family (rather than the family’s existence itself) remains an inference from the compiled document, not a direct citation.
